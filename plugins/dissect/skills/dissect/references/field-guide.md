# Field guide

Vocabulary and rough benchmarks for dissecting videos. Use the names below so blueprints read consistently, but don't force a label that doesn't fit. Describe what you see instead.

Contents: 1. Reading the numbers · 2. Hooks · 3. Beats · 4. Retention devices · 5. Shots and camera · 6. Captions and on-screen text · 7. Transitions · 8. Sound · 9. Packaging · 10. Signature vs format

## 1. Reading the numbers

These are rough ranges from common practice, not rules. Genres and platforms vary, so use them to say "fast for a long-form explainer", not to grade.

| Measure | Slow | Typical | Fast |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cuts per minute, long-form talking head | < 6 | 6–15 | 20+ (jump-cut heavy) |
| Cuts per minute, Shorts / Reels / TikTok | < 10 | 15–35 | 40+ |
| Picture changes per minute (cuts + on-screen steps) | < 10 | 15–30 | 40+ |
| Average shot length | > 6 s | 2–5 s | < 1.5 s |
| First picture change (short-form) | > 3 s | 1–2 s | < 1 s |
| Words per minute | < 140 (calm narration) | 150–180 (conversational) | 190+ (hype, dense explainers) |

- **Speech covering 95%+ of the video** means talk-driven and probably jump-cut, with breaths removed. Lower coverage means room for music, visuals, beats or silence.
- **Pauses of 0.6 s or more** in otherwise fast speech are usually deliberate. Look at the word that follows: often a number, a reveal or a punchline.
- **Pitch**: a median under ~130 Hz is a low register, 130–200 Hz medium, over 200 Hz high. Movement (robust spread) under 2 semitones sounds flat, 2–3.5 natural, over 3.5 very animated.
- **Sentence endings**: mostly falling sounds assured. Frequent rises sound conversational, questioning or uptalky.
- **Loudness**: YouTube and most streaming platforms play back around −14 LUFS. Short-form is often mastered hotter, around −12 to −9. A true peak of about −1 dBFS or lower avoids clipping after encoding.
- **Music bed under speech**: 15–25 dB below the voice is a classic narration mix. Within 10 dB is music-forward. Near-silent pauses mean a dry voice with no bed.
- **Engagement** (platform-dependent, so compare within a platform):
  - **Views per follower above 1**: the video travelled beyond the existing audience.
  - **Likes per 1,000 views**: often 10–40 on YouTube, usually higher on TikTok and Reels.
  - **Comments per 1,000 views**: around 0.5–3.
- **Most-replayed peaks** (YouTube) mark what people rewind to: a payoff, a visual gag, a fact on screen, or confusion. Say which.

## 2. Hooks

Name the type, then write a reusable template line.

| Type | What it does | Template |
|---|---|---|
| Shocking fact or number | A concrete surprise in the first line | "{{THING}} {{does something that sounds impossible}}." |
| Contrarian claim | Contradicts a belief | "Everything you know about {{TOPIC}} is wrong." |
| Curiosity gap | Names a mystery, withholds the answer | "There's a reason {{X}}, and it's not what you think." |
| Question | Asks what the viewer already wonders | "Why does {{X}} {{Y}}?" |
| Stakes | What the viewer stands to lose or gain | "If you {{do X}}, you're losing {{Y}}." |
| Call-out | Selects the audience | "If you're a {{GROUP}}, stop scrolling." |
| Challenge / experiment | Sets up a test with an outcome | "I tried {{X}} for {{N}} days." |
| Result first | Shows the end state, then how | Final reveal in frame 1, then "here's how". |
| In medias res | Starts mid-action, no intro | Cold open on the most dramatic moment. |
| Visual pattern interrupt | An unexpected image or motion in frame 1 | Object pushed at the lens, a fast zoom, an impossible image. |
| List promise | Signals a structure and its length | "{{N}} {{things}} that {{benefit}}." |
| Authority / proof | Earns attention with credentials or results | "I've {{done X}}. Here's what nobody tells you." |

Hooks usually stack two or three of these (picture + line + on-screen text). Note which layer does what.

## 3. Beats

Hook · promise (what you'll get) · context / setup · stakes (why it matters) · explanation step · example or demo · twist / turn ("but here's the catch") · escalation · payoff / reveal · recap · call to action · bridge or loop back to the start · teaser for the next video.

A beat sheet row should say what happens *and* the technique, e.g. "Stakes: personal-cost framing, 'you' + a rupee figure".

## 4. Retention devices

- **Open loop**: raise a question early and answer it late. Note where it's opened and where it closes.
- **Re-hook**: a fresh mini-promise every 20–40 s in long-form, every 5–10 s in short-form.
- **Progress signal**: "number 3", a step counter, a progress bar.
- **Pattern interrupt**: a sudden change of picture, sound, angle or pace.
- **Escalation**: each beat bigger than the last.
- **Specificity**: exact numbers, names and dates beat vague claims.
- **Second person**: "you" ties the topic to the viewer.
- **Redundancy for silent viewing**: the key words also appear on screen.
- **Show, don't tell**: every noun gets a picture.
- **Loop ending**: the last line flows into the first, so replays feel seamless (short-form).
- **Cliffhanger CTA**: the call to action comes before the final payoff, or points to the full video.

## 5. Shots and camera

- **Types:**
  - presenter: talking head (A-roll), walk-and-talk, UGC / selfie, POV, reaction insert
  - footage: b-roll (own, stock, archival), product close-up, drone / establishing
  - designed: screen recording, motion graphic / infographic, kinetic typography, whiteboard / doodle, AI-generated image or clip
  - composited: meme insert, map, split screen, green screen
- **Framing:** extreme close-up · close-up · medium close-up · medium · wide. Angle: eye level, low, high, top-down.
- **Motion:** static (locked off) · handheld · push-in (slow digital zoom) · punch-in (instant zoom on the cut) · pan / tilt · tracking · whip · speed ramp.
- **Shot mix** is the share of running time per type. Estimate it from the shot list and sheets, then state it in the format card.

## 6. Captions and on-screen text

- **Caption styles:**
  - word-by-word karaoke (the active word highlighted)
  - 1–3 word chunks
  - full-sentence subtitles
  - keyword pops (only key words, large)
  - emoji-augmented captions
  - headline cards (a title per segment)
- **Describe:** font look and closest free match, weight, case, size relative to the frame, fill / outline / shadow / box, highlight colour and rule (which words get it), position (centre, lower third, top), words per card, animation (pop, bounce, typewriter, fade), timing (on the word, or ahead of it).
- **Common closest free matches** (Google Fonts): Montserrat (ExtraBold / Black), Poppins, Inter, Anton, Bebas Neue, Oswald, Archivo Black, Luckiest Guy (playful), Playfair Display / Libre Baskerville (editorial serif), Space Mono / IBM Plex Mono (technical labels).
- **Other text:** titles, lower-thirds, labels, source lines, stamps, counters, callouts with arrows or circles.

## 7. Transitions

Hard cut · jump cut (same shot, time removed) · punch-in cut · J-cut / L-cut (audio leads or trails the picture) · match cut · dissolve / cross-fade · whip pan · zoom transition · glitch · light leak · shape or mask wipe · page turn. Note how often each appears and whether it lands on a beat, a word or a sound effect.

## 8. Sound

- **Music:** genre and mood (lo-fi, cinematic, trap, upbeat pop, ambient, orchestral), tempo, level under the voice, where it enters, drops out, swells or changes. Platform metadata often names the track on TikTok and Reels.
- **Effects:** whoosh (on cuts, moves), hit / impact (on reveals), riser (before reveals), pop / click (text appearing), swish, ding, record scratch, camera shutter, typing, crowd.
- **Space:** dramatic silence, music drop before a punchline, room tone.
- **Voice processing** (inferred): close-mic intimacy vs room sound, compression (even loudness), brightness.

## 9. Packaging

- **Title formulas:** curiosity ("Why {{X}}"), number ("{{N}} {{things}}"), challenge ("I {{did X}}"), contrast ("{{X}} vs {{Y}}"), stakes ("{{X}} is about to {{Y}}"), question.
- **Thumbnail / cover patterns:** face with a strong emotion + 2–4 words, before / after, one giant object, an arrow or circle on a detail, high contrast, a big number, the most-replayed moment as the cover.
- **Description and comments:** first-line hook, links to the full video, hashtags (how many, broad vs niche), pinned comment or question to drive replies.

## 10. Signature vs format

Separate these explicitly in every blueprint:
- **Signature (theirs):** face, name, voice, catchphrases, recurring characters or bits, signature music, logo and brand palette, unique access (people, places, money).
- **Format (reusable):** structure, beat timing, hook mechanics, pacing, caption system, shot grammar, sound design, packaging pattern.
